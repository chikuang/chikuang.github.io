The statistical lens#

For each new idea, ask: What are the observations? What is random? What is the target? What is being optimized? What assumptions justify the evaluation? The connections below are learning aids; their limits matter as much as their similarities.

A translation guide#

GenAI concept

Statistical starting point

What needs care

Language modeling

Conditional density estimation and the chain rule

Token likelihood measures text prediction, not the truth of a claim.

Cross-entropy training

Maximum likelihood and empirical risk minimization

Token observations within documents are dependent; split and evaluate at an appropriate unit.

Embeddings

Learned feature maps and low-dimensional representations

Coordinates are learned representations, not uniquely identified scientific factors.

Attention

Query-dependent weighted averages, reminiscent of kernel smoothing

Queries, keys, and values are learned; attention weights are not automatically causal importance or posterior probabilities.

Transformer

A composition of learned feature transformations

Attention is one component alongside positional information, nonlinear layers, and residual connections.

Temperature

A transformation of a categorical sampling distribution

More or less randomness does not by itself improve calibration or factual correctness.

Variational autoencoder

Latent-variable modeling and variational inference

The encoder approximates a posterior under the fitted model; approximation and model misspecification both matter.

Diffusion

Denoising, score estimation, and stochastic processes

A learned reverse process approximates a distribution; it does not recover a unique original observation from noise.

LoRA

A low-rank restriction on parameter updates

This is a constraint on an update matrix, not necessarily on the entire fitted model.

Preference optimization

Paired-comparison models and regularized decision making

Annotator preferences and reward proxies are not objective truth.

RAG

Retrieval followed by prediction conditional on selected evidence

Retrieved documents are selected, potentially incomplete or wrong, and usually not random samples from a posterior.

Agent

Sequential decisions with actions, observations, and a stopping rule

The language model is only one part of the system; tools, environment, and accumulated errors also matter.

LLM evaluation

Measurement, experimental design, and uncertainty quantification

Repeated answers to one prompt do not create independent test tasks.

Three equations to keep in view#

A language model is a distribution over sequences#

For tokens \(x_1,\ldots,x_T\),

\[ p_\theta(x_{1:T})=\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t}). \]

Training commonly minimizes the sum of negative log conditional probabilities. Generation then samples from a conditional distribution, often modified by a decoding rule. Training and decoding answer different questions.

Attention forms a data-dependent weighted average#

For one attention head,

\[ A=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}+M\right), \qquad Z=AV, \]

where softmax is applied row by row and \(M\) can mask unavailable positions. With \(Q\in\mathbb R^{n_q\times d_k}\), \(K\in\mathbb R^{n_k\times d_k}\), and \(V\in\mathbb R^{n_k\times d_v}\), the output is \(Z\in\mathbb R^{n_q\times d_v}\). Each row of \(A\) sums to one over allowed positions. This invites a comparison to a normalized smoother, while the learned representations and end-to-end training make it different from a fixed kernel estimator. See Vaswani et al..

Evaluate a system on a specified task distribution#

\[ R(\pi)=\mathbb E_{\tau\sim\mathcal D}\!\left[L(\pi,\tau)\right]. \]

Here \(\pi\) is the full system, \(\tau\) a task, and \(L\) a loss that may include failure, cost, and latency. Specify the task population and sampling design before reporting a performance estimate. For stochastic systems, distinguish between-task variation from variation across repeated runs of the same task.

Questions for every paper or experiment#

  1. What is the estimand or target behavior, and what is actually measured?

  2. What are the training, validation, and test units? Where could leakage enter?

  3. Which quantities are learned, fixed, sampled, or selected adaptively?

  4. What simple baseline would tell us whether the additional complexity helps?

  5. How would distribution shift, dependence, or misspecification change the conclusion?

  6. Which claims are empirically supported, and which remain useful analogies?
