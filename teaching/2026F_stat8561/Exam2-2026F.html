Question 1: Multiple Choice [50]

Each part is worth [5] points. Select the single best answer under the stated conditions. Record it on the separate answer sheet. No explanation is required.

1a)

Two OLS mean models are \(M_A:\mu_A(x)=\alpha_0+\alpha_1x\) and \(M_B:\mu_B(z)=\gamma_0+\gamma_1z\). On the same observations, \(\mathbf1\), \(\mathbf x\), and \(\mathbf z\) are linearly independent, and \(\mathrm{SSE}_A=84\), \(\mathrm{SSE}_B=72\). Which statement is correct?

A. The usual partial \(F\) test comparing A directly with B has one added degree of freedom.
B. The lower \(\mathrm{SSE}_B\) establishes that \(z\) contributes after accounting for \(x\).
C. Neither mean space contains the other, so their SSE difference does not give a valid nested-model \(F\) test.
D. The SSE values must be equal because both models have two coefficients.

TipAnswer

Answer: C. The independence of \(\mathbf1\), \(\mathbf x\), and \(\mathbf z\) means neither two-dimensional mean space contains the other. The partial \(F\) formula requires nested models, not merely two SSE values. To test \(z\) after \(x\), compare \(\alpha_0+\alpha_1x\) with \(\beta_0+\beta_1x+\beta_2z\).

1b)

Consider \(\mu(x,z)=\beta_0+\beta_xx+\beta_gz+\beta_{xg}xz\), with \(z=0\) or \(1\). Rewrite the same model using \(x_c=x-5\), retaining all terms. Which statement is correct?

A. The coefficient of \(z\) becomes \(\beta_g+5\beta_{xg}\); fitted values and SSE are unchanged.
B. The coefficient of \(z\) must remain \(\beta_g\), even when \(\beta_{xg}\ne0\).
C. Centering changes the fitted mean for each observation.
D. The coefficient of \(x_cz\) becomes \(5\beta_{xg}\).

TipAnswer

Answer: A. Substitute \(x=x_c+5\). The coefficient of \(z\) is \(\beta_g+5\beta_{xg}\), the group difference \(\Delta(5)\). The intercept becomes \(\beta_0+5\beta_x\), while the slope difference remains \(\beta_{xg}\). Centering moves the reference point; fitted values, residuals, and SSE stay the same.

1c)

For the same observations, consider \(M_0:\mu=\alpha_0\), \(M_1:\mu=\alpha_0+\alpha_1x_1\), and \(M_2:\mu=\beta_0+\beta_1x_1+\beta_2x_2\). Which comparison tests whether \(x_2\) contributes to the mean after accounting for \(x_1\)?

A. \(M_0\) versus \(M_2\).
B. \(M_1\) versus \(M_2\).
C. \(M_0\) versus \(M_1\).
D. Compare only the two fitted coefficients of \(x_1\).

TipAnswer

Answer: B. The reduced model retains \(x_1\) and removes \(x_2\). The partial \(F\) test addresses \(H_0:\beta_2=0\) in the full model: does \(x_2\) contribute after accounting for \(x_1\)? It does not test equality of the two fitted \(x_1\) coefficients.

1d)

Two full-rank nested OLS models use the same observations and include an intercept, with \(\mathrm{SST}>0\) and \(\mathrm{SSE}_F>0\). Adding one coefficient gives partial \(F=0.8\). Which change from the reduced to the full model follows? (The formulas are given in 3a.)

A. Both \(R^2\) and \(R^2_{\mathrm{adj}}\) increase.
B. \(R^2\) decreases, while \(R^2_{\mathrm{adj}}\) increases.
C. Both measures remain unchanged because \(F<1\).
D. \(R^2\) increases, while \(R^2_{\mathrm{adj}}\) decreases.

TipAnswer

Answer: D. Here \(F>0\), so \(\mathrm{SSE}_R>\mathrm{SSE}_F\) and \(R^2\) increases. For one added coefficient, \(F<1\) means the SSE reduction is less than \(\mathrm{MSE}_F\), so \(\mathrm{MSE}_R<\mathrm{MSE}_F\). With the same SST, adjusted \(R^2\) therefore decreases.

1e)

Two predictors \(x\) and \(z\) are correlated. In sequential ANOVA on the same observations, the sum of squares for \(z\) is 45 when \(z\) enters first, but 12 when it enters after \(x\). Why can both values be correct?

A. They use the same reduced model but different estimates of the error variance.
B. They use different reduced models: intercept only versus intercept plus \(x\).
C. They estimate the noise variance before and after adjustment for \(x\).
D. They use different final fits because reversing the term order changes fitted values.

TipAnswer

Answer: B. A sequential sum of squares is the reduction in SSE when a term is added after the preceding terms. Adding \(z\) first compares intercept only with intercept plus \(z\); adding it after \(x\) compares intercept plus \(x\) with intercept plus \(x\) and \(z\). Correlated predictors can give different reductions, while the final fitted values are unchanged.

1f)

A group-only model uses A as reference: \(\mathbb E(Y_i\mid G_i)=\beta_0+\beta_BD_{Bi}+\beta_CD_{Ci}\), where \(D_{Bi}\) and \(D_{Ci}\) indicate membership in B and C. All three groups are observed. With B as the new reference, which intercept and C coefficient preserve the same group means?

A. Intercept \(\beta_0\); C coefficient \(\beta_C\).
B. Intercept \(\beta_B\); C coefficient \(\beta_C\).
C. Intercept \(\beta_0+\beta_B\); C coefficient \(\beta_C\).
D. Intercept \(\beta_0+\beta_B\); C coefficient \(\beta_C-\beta_B\).

TipAnswer

Answer: D. With A as reference, \(\mu_A=\beta_0\), \(\mu_B=\beta_0+\beta_B\), and \(\mu_C=\beta_0+\beta_C\). With B as reference, the intercept is \(\mu_B\) and the C coefficient is \(\mu_C-\mu_B=\beta_C-\beta_B\). The coefficient meanings change, while fitted group means, residuals, and SSE stay the same.

1g)

Let \(z=0\) for group A, \(z=1\) for group B, and \(x_c=x-2\). A fitted interaction model is \(\widehat\mu(x,z)=12+3x_c+5z-x_cz\). Which pair gives the fitted slope for B and the B-minus-A fitted mean difference at \(x=4\), respectively?

A. Slope \(2\); mean difference \(3\).
B. Slope \(-1\); mean difference \(3\).
C. Slope \(2\); mean difference \(5\).
D. Slope \(8\); mean difference \(3\).

TipAnswer

Answer: A. Setting \(z=0\) gives slope 3; setting \(z=1\) gives slope \(3-1=2\). The B-minus-A fitted mean difference is \(\widehat\Delta(x)=5-(x-2)\), giving 3 at \(x=4\). The negative interaction means this gap decreases with \(x\), while both fitted slopes remain positive.

1h)

For full-rank OLS, \(\hat{\mathbf Y}=\mathbf H\mathbf Y\) and \(\mathbf e=(\mathbf I-\mathbf H)\mathbf Y\). Keep \(\mathbf X\) fixed and increase only \(Y_i\) by 10. If \(h_{ii}=0.8\), which statement must hold after refitting?

A. The raw residual \(e_i\) increases by 10, and \(h_{ii}\) is unchanged.
B. The raw residual \(e_i\) increases by 2, and \(h_{ii}\) increases.
C. The raw residual \(e_i\) increases by 2, and \(h_{ii}\) is unchanged.
D. The fitted value \(\hat Y_i\) is unchanged, and \(e_i\) increases by 8.

TipAnswer

Answer: C. The hat matrix depends only on \(\mathbf X\), so \(h_{ii}\) is unchanged. Row \(i\) of \(\hat{\mathbf Y}=\mathbf H\mathbf Y\) gives a fitted-value increase of \(10h_{ii}=8\). Hence \(e_i=Y_i-\hat Y_i\) increases by \(10-8=2\), rather than by the full response change.

1i)

With an intercept and one nonconstant predictor, \(h_{ii}=1/n+(x_i-\bar x)^2/\sum_j(x_j-\bar x)^2\). Replace every \(x_i\) by \(u_i=10+2x_i\) and refit with an intercept to the same observations. What happens to leverage?

A. Every leverage doubles because the predictor is multiplied by 2.
B. Every leverage is divided by 4.
C. Adding 10 makes every observation have higher leverage.
D. Every leverage is unchanged.

TipAnswer

Answer: D. The new mean is \(\bar u=10+2\bar x\), so \(u_i-\bar u=2(x_i-\bar x)\). Both the squared numerator and the sum in the denominator multiply by 4; the ratio and \(1/n\) are unchanged. A change of predictor origin or units does not change leverage.

1j)

An observation has the largest response \(Y_i\) in the sample, but \(Y_i=\hat Y_i\). Assume \(h_{ii}<1\), \(s>0\), and \(s_{(-i)}>0\). Which conclusion follows?

A. It must be a response outlier because its response is largest.
B. \(e_i=r_i=t_i=0\); its response size alone does not make it a response outlier.
C. Its leverage must be zero because its residual is zero.
D. Its standardized residual must be large because \(Y_i\) is large.

TipAnswer

Answer: B. A response outlier is unusual given its predictor values and the specified model. Here the raw residual \(e_i=Y_i-\hat Y_i=0\), so the standardized residual \(r_i\) and externally studentized residual \(t_i\) are also zero. An extreme response alone does not make an observation an outlier; zero residual does not imply zero leverage.

Question 2: True or False [20]

Each part is worth [4] points. Record T or F on the separate answer sheet. No explanation is required.

2a)

For a nested normal-model comparison on the same observations that adds one coefficient, the partial \(F\) statistic equals \(T^2\), where \(T\) is the corresponding two-sided \(t\)-test statistic from the full model, regardless of its sign.

TipAnswer

Answer: True. For one coefficient restriction, \(F=T^2\), as in Chapter 3. Squaring removes the sign. Both tests address the same null hypothesis; the \(F\) reference degrees of freedom are \((1,n-p_F)\).

2b)

With all four groups observed, an intercept plus all four group indicators gives five identifiable coefficients in a group-only regression model.

TipAnswer

Answer: False. The four indicators sum to the intercept column, so the design has rank 4 rather than 5. Use an intercept plus three indicators or all four indicators without a separate intercept.

2c)

For the additive linear mean model \(\mu(x_1,x_2)=\beta_0+\beta_1x_1+\beta_2x_2\), the difference \(\mu(x_1+1,x_2)-\mu(x_1,x_2)\) equals \(\beta_1+\beta_2x_2\).

TipAnswer

Answer: False. Holding \(x_2\) fixed, the intercept and unchanged contribution \(\beta_2x_2\) cancel. The mean-response difference is \(\beta_1\). Additivity means this difference does not depend on \(x_2\); it does not require independent predictors.

2d)

For a full-rank design with \(p\) columns, \(\sum_i h_{ii}=p\) implies that every observation has leverage \(p/n\).

TipAnswer

Answer: False. Because \(\sum_i h_{ii}=p\), average leverage is \(p/n\). Individual leverages can differ because they describe the positions of the predictor rows. The identity does not make each leverage equal to the average.

2e)

Convert every response from centimeters to meters and refit the same OLS model, keeping \(\mathbf X\) fixed. If \(s>0\) and all \(h_{ii}<1\), every standardized residual \(r_i\) is unchanged.

TipAnswer

Answer: True. OLS fitted values, raw residuals, and \(s\) all divide by 100, while \(\mathbf H\) and \(h_{ii}\) stay the same. Thus \(r_i=(e_i/100)/\{(s/100)\sqrt{1-h_{ii}}\}\) is unchanged. The numerical response units cancel.

Question 3: Short Answer [30]

Each part is worth [10] points. Show brief calculations and concise reasoning on the separate answer sheet.

3a) Better fit versus evidence [10]

Two full-rank nested OLS models with intercepts use the same \(n=28\) observations, with \(\mathrm{SST}>0\). The reduced model has \(p_R=2\), \(\mathrm{SSE}_R=84\); the full model has \(p_F=4\), \(\mathrm{SSE}_F=72\). Assume independent normal errors with common variance under the full model. Use

\[ F=\frac{(\mathrm{SSE}_R-\mathrm{SSE}_F)/(p_F-p_R)}{\mathrm{SSE}_F/(n-p_F)},\qquad R^2_{\mathrm{adj}}=1-\frac{\mathrm{SSE}/(n-p)}{\mathrm{SST}/(n-1)}. \]

3a(i) [4]

Compute \(F\) and give its null reference degrees of freedom.

TipAnswer

Answer: \(F=\{(84-72)/(4-2)\}/\{72/(28-4)\}=6/3=2\) [2]. Under the reduced-model null, \(F\sim F_{2,24}\) [2].

3a(ii) [6]

A classmate claims, “Adjusted \(R^2\) increases, so we must reject the reduced model at 5%.” Assess both parts using the formulas; only the direction of change in adjusted \(R^2\) is needed. The 5% upper-tail critical value is 3.40. Explain why the two criteria need not agree.

TipAnswer

Answer: The same positive \(\mathrm{SST}/(n-1)\) is used for both fits. Since \(\mathrm{MSE}_R=84/26\approx3.231>3=\mathrm{MSE}_F\), adjusted \(R^2\) increases [2]. However, \(2<3.40\), so fail to reject the reduced model at 5% [2]. Adjusted \(R^2\) compares residual mean squares; the test asks whether improvement is sufficiently large relative to sampling variation under the null [2]. In fact, adjusted \(R^2\) increases when \(F>1\), a weaker condition than \(F>3.40\). The classmate’s implication is false.

3b) Agreement at one point or everywhere [10]

Let \(z=0\) for group A, \(z=1\) for group B, and \(x_c=x-8\). Consider the full-rank interaction model

\[ \mu(x,z)=\beta_0+\beta_xx_c+\beta_gz+\beta_{xg}x_cz. \]

3b(i) [4]

Write \(\Delta(x)=\mu_B(x)-\mu_A(x)\) and the null restriction for equal group means at \(x=10\).

TipAnswer

Answer: Subtracting the two group mean functions gives \(\Delta(x)=\mu_B(x)-\mu_A(x)=\beta_g+\beta_{xg}(x-8)\) [2]. Equality at \(x=10\) is the single restriction \(\beta_g+2\beta_{xg}=0\) [2].

3b(ii) [6]

Give and verify one numerical pair \((\beta_g,\beta_{xg})\) for which the group means agree at \(x=10\) but the two lines are not identical. Then state and justify the coefficient restrictions implied by agreement at both \(x=6\) and \(x=10\).

TipAnswer

Answer: For example, \(\beta_g=-2\) and \(\beta_{xg}=1\) give \(\Delta(10)=0\) but \(\Delta(8)=-2\), so the lines intersect without being identical [2]. Equality at 6 and 10 gives \(\beta_g-2\beta_{xg}=0\) and \(\beta_g+2\beta_{xg}=0\). Subtraction yields \(4\beta_{xg}=0\); then \(\beta_g=0\) [2 for the restrictions, 2 for the reasoning]. Thus two distinct points of agreement force these two straight mean lines to be identical.

3c) Which residual answers the question? [10]

Use the Section 6.3 standardized and externally studentized residuals:

\[ r_i=\frac{e_i}{s\sqrt{1-h_{ii}}},\qquad t_i=\frac{e_i}{s_{(-i)}\sqrt{1-h_{ii}}}. \]

One full fit has \(n=9\), \(p=4\), \(s=2\), and the following diagnostics:

Observation Raw residual \(e_i\) Leverage \(h_{ii}\)
A 2 0.75
B 3 0.36

3c(i) [6]

Compute \(r_A\) and \(r_B\). Which is more unusual by absolute standardized residual? Explain why the larger raw residual gives the opposite ranking.

TipAnswer

Answer: \(r_A=2/(2\sqrt{0.25})=2\) [2]; \(r_B=3/(2\sqrt{0.64})=1.875\) [2]. A has the larger absolute standardized residual [1], despite its smaller raw residual: A’s larger leverage makes its denominator smaller [1].

3c(ii) [4]

Refitting without A gives \(s_{(-A)}=1\) and retains full column rank. Compute \(t_A\). A student attributes \(|t_A|>|r_A|\) to a deleted-fit residual in the numerator. Correct the explanation: identify what changes and what stays the same.

TipAnswer

Answer: \(t_A=2/(1\sqrt{0.25})=4\) [2]. The claim is incorrect: the numerator remains the full-fit raw residual \(e_A\), and leverage also comes from the full fit [1]. Only the residual SD is replaced, from \(s=2\) to \(s_{(-A)}=1\), doubling the scaled residual [1].
