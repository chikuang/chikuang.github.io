Question 1: Multiple Choice [54]

Select the single best answer. Each part is worth 6 points.

1a)

A target distribution has CDF \(F(x)=1-(1-x)^3\) for \(0<x<1\), with \(F(x)=0\) for \(x\leq0\) and \(F(x)=1\) for \(x\geq1\). For \(U\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)\), which is its inverse-CDF generator?

A. \(X=1-(1-U)^3\).
B. \(X=1-(1-U)^{1/3}\).
C. \(X=U^3\).
D. \(X=3U(1-U)\).

TipAnswer

Answer: B. Solving \(u=1-(1-x)^3\) gives \(x=1-(1-u)^{1/3}\).

1b)

In rejection sampling with target density \(f\) and proposal density \(g\), which support condition is required?

A. The target and proposal must have exactly the same support.
B. The proposal must be uniform on its support.
C. \(g(x)>0\) wherever \(f(x)>0\).
D. The proposal must have a smaller mean than the target.

TipAnswer

Answer: C. The proposal density must be positive wherever the target density is positive, so every part of the target support is accessible.

1c)

Let \(U_1,U_2,U_3\) be independent \(\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)\) variables. What is the distribution of \(T=-\log(U_1U_2U_3)/2\)?

A. \(\operatorname{Gamma}(3,2)\).
B. \(\operatorname{Gamma}(2,3)\).
C. \(\operatorname{Gamma}(3,1/2)\).
D. Exponential with rate 6.

TipAnswer

Answer: A. Each \(-\log U_j/2\) is exponential with rate 2. The independent sum has gamma shape 3 and rate 2.

1d)

Let \(\mathbf Z\sim N_p(\mathbf0,I_p)\) and let the upper triangular matrix \(R\) satisfy \(R^\top R=\Sigma\). Which construction always has distribution \(N_p(\boldsymbol\mu,\Sigma)\)?

A. \(\mathbf X=\boldsymbol\mu+R\mathbf Z\).
B. \(\mathbf X=\boldsymbol\mu+\Sigma\mathbf Z\).
C. \(\mathbf X=R^\top(\boldsymbol\mu+\mathbf Z)\).
D. \(\mathbf X=\boldsymbol\mu+R^\top\mathbf Z\).

TipAnswer

Answer: D. \(\operatorname{Cov}(R^\top\mathbf Z)=R^\top I_pR=\Sigma\) and the mean is \(\boldsymbol\mu\). Here the random vectors are columns.

1e)

Two batches of independent outputs from the same model have sample sizes 100 and 900, with means 2.0 and 3.0, respectively. What is the mean of all 1,000 outputs?

A. 2.5.
B. 2.9.
C. 5.0.
D. It cannot be determined without the two sample variances.

TipAnswer

Answer: B. Pooling the observations gives \((100\cdot2+900\cdot3)/1000=2.9\). The two batch means must be weighted by their batch sizes.

1f)

Let \(E[Y]=\theta\), \(E[C]=3\), and \(Z=Y-2(C-2.5)\). What is \(E[Z]\)?

A. \(\theta-1\).
B. \(\theta\).
C. \(\theta+1\).
D. \(\theta-5\).

TipAnswer

Answer: A. By linearity, \(E[Z]=\theta-2(3-2.5)=\theta-1\).

1g)

Let \(g(u)=(u-1/2)^2\) and \(U\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)\). Note that \(g(1-u)=g(u)\). Compare the mean of \(K\) independent pair averages \(\{g(U_k)+g(1-U_k)\}/2\) with the mean of \(2K\) independent \(g(U)\) outputs. For the same \(2K\) function evaluations, the antithetic estimator’s variance is:

A. Zero.
B. Half the independent estimator’s variance.
C. Equal to the independent estimator’s variance.
D. Twice the independent estimator’s variance.

TipAnswer

Answer: D. \(g(1-u)=g(u)\), so each pair average is just \(g(U)\). With \(2K\) function calls, the antithetic variance is \(\sigma_g^2/K\), whereas the independent variance is \(\sigma_g^2/(2K)\).

1h)

Let \(\widehat\theta_N\) be the mean of \(N\) independent, identically distributed simulation outputs with finite mean \(\theta\). Which statement about Monte Carlo error is correct?

A. \(|\widehat\theta_N-\theta|\) must decrease whenever one more output is added.
B. The estimate equals \(\theta\) exactly once \(N\) is sufficiently large.
C. The absolute error can increase after one more output is added, even though the law of large numbers applies.
D. Doubling \(N\) makes the realized absolute error exactly half as large.

TipAnswer

Answer: C. Convergence of the average does not mean that its absolute error decreases at every step. A new observation can move an estimate farther from its target.

1i)

In 5-fold CV for a fixed candidate model, which data are used to fit the model that predicts observations in the held-out fold?

A. Only the held-out fold.
B. Only the other four folds.
C. All five development folds.
D. The separate test set.

TipAnswer

Answer: B. When one fold is held out, its predictions come from a fit using only the other four folds.

Question 2: True or False [18]

Write True or False. Each part is worth 3 points; no explanation is required.

2a)

A gamma distribution with shape \(2\) and rate \(3\) is the same distribution as a gamma distribution with shape \(2\) and scale \(3\).

TipAnswer

Answer: False. A gamma rate of 3 corresponds to scale \(1/3\), not scale 3.

2b)

A discrete distribution can be generated from a \(\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)\) draw by partitioning \((0,1)\) into intervals whose lengths equal the category probabilities and returning the category of the interval containing the draw.

TipAnswer

Answer: True. A uniform draw falls in each interval with probability equal to its length, giving the specified category probabilities.

2c)

If independent simulation outputs have variance 9, then the average of 900 such outputs has Monte Carlo standard error 0.1, although an individual output has standard deviation 3.

TipAnswer

Answer: True. The output SD is 3, while the SD of the average of 900 independent outputs is \(3/\sqrt{900}=0.1\).

2d)

If an unbiased control-variate estimator has smaller variance than the plain Monte Carlo estimator, its realized value must be closer to the target in every simulation run.

TipAnswer

Answer: False. Variance is a repeated-run property. Smaller variance does not force every realized estimate to be closer to \(\theta\).

2e)

In ordinary \(K\)-fold CV without repetitions, for each candidate model, each development observation is used for validation once and belongs to the training set for the other \(K-1\) fits.

TipAnswer

Answer: True. Each observation belongs to one validation fold and is available for training in the other \(K-1\) fits, for each candidate.

2f)

For a polynomial model, the degree \(d\) and the coefficients \(\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_d\) are all estimated by least squares in each CV fit.

TipAnswer

Answer: False. The degree \(d\) is a candidate hyperparameter held fixed within each CV fit; least squares estimates the coefficients \(\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_d\) from that fit’s training observations. CV compares candidate degrees.

Question 3: Short Answers [28]

Answer each part separately. Brief formulas and one or two sentences per part are sufficient.

3a) Rejection sampling [10]

The target density is \(f(x)=2(1-x)\) on \(0<x<1\) and zero otherwise. The proposal is uniform on \((0,1)\), with density \(g(x)=1\) there.

3a(i) [4]

Find the smallest envelope constant \(M_{\min}\) satisfying \(f(x)\leq M_{\min}g(x)\) for every \(0<x<1\), and state its overall acceptance probability.

TipAnswer

Answer: \(M_{\min}=2\), and the overall acceptance probability is \(1/M_{\min}=1/2\). The ratio \(f(x)/g(x)=2(1-x)\) has supremum 2 on \((0,1)\). No proof of the rejection algorithm is required.

3a(ii) [6]

For 400 independent proposals, calculate the expected number of accepted draws using \(M=4\) and using \(M=M_{\min}\). Which choice is more efficient at this proposal budget, and by what factor?

TipAnswer

Answer: The expected accepted counts are \(400/4=100\) for \(M=4\) and \(400/2=200\) for \(M=M_{\min}=2\). The smaller constant \(M=2\) is more efficient, yielding twice as many accepted draws for the same proposal budget.

3b) Two control adjustments [10]

Let \(E[Y]=\theta\), \(E[C]=\mu_C\) (known), \(\operatorname{Var}(Y)=4\), \(\operatorname{Var}(C)=1\), and \(\operatorname{Cov}(Y,C)=1.5\). Consider \(Z_A=Y-(C-\mu_C)\) and \(Z_B=Y+(C-\mu_C)\). Each estimator averages \(N\) independent \((Y,C)\) pairs, using the same \(N\).

You may use

\[ \operatorname{Var}(Y+cC)=\operatorname{Var}(Y)+c^2\operatorname{Var}(C)+2c\operatorname{Cov}(Y,C). \]

3b(i) [4]

State \(E[Z_A]\) and \(E[Z_B]\), and explain why.

TipAnswer

Answer: Both \(E[Z_A]=\theta\) and \(E[Z_B]=\theta\), because \(E[C-\mu_C]=0\). The sign affects precision without changing the expectation.

3b(ii) [6]

Calculate \(\operatorname{Var}(Z_A)\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(Z_B)\). Which adjustment improves precision over the plain mean of \(Y\), and why?

TipAnswer

Answer:

\[ \operatorname{Var}(Z_A)=4+1-2(1.5)=2,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(Z_B)=4+1+2(1.5)=8. \]

Choose A. For \(N\) independent pairs, the estimator variances are \(2/N\) and \(8/N\), compared with \(4/N\) for the plain mean. Thus A improves precision, whereas B reduces precision. The optimal coefficient is not requested.

3c) Held-out prediction error [8]

A dataset has 20 independent observations, divided into four equal folds. Two candidate models use the same folds. The table gives the sum of squared held-out prediction errors in each fold, not the fold MSE.

Model Fold 1 Fold 2 Fold 3 Fold 4
A 4 8 6 2
B 5 5 7 7

3c(i) [4]

Compute the overall CV MSE for each model, giving every observation equal weight. Show the denominator used.

TipAnswer

Answer:

\[ \operatorname{CV}(A)=\frac{4+8+6+2}{20}=1,\qquad \operatorname{CV}(B)=\frac{5+5+7+7}{20}=1.2. \]

Divide each total squared error by all 20 held-out observations. Equivalently, average the four fold MSEs, since all folds contain five observations.

3c(ii) [4]

Model B has the smaller training MSE. Which model should be selected by CV, and why does the smaller training MSE not determine this choice?

TipAnswer

Answer: Select A, since it has the smaller held-out MSE. Training error uses the data that fitted the model and can favour fitting noise. A smaller training MSE need not imply better prediction on new observations, so it does not determine this CV choice.
